# Reading Comprehension

Reading Comprehension is part of the GRE Verbal Reasoning section. The main task is not just to understand the topic of a passage, but to understand the author's argument, structure, evidence, assumptions, and details.

GRE Reading Comprehension passages can be short or long. The best strategy changes depending on the length of the passage and the number of questions connected to it.

---

## How Reading Comprehension Works on the GRE

Short passages usually have less text and fewer questions attached to them. Because the time investment is smaller, you should often let the question guide your reading.

Long passages take more time, but they often support several questions. Because multiple questions can come from the same passage, it can be worth spending more time understanding the passage carefully at the beginning.

The important GRE idea is this:

```
Do not use the same reading strategy for every passage.
```

Short passages and long passages reward different approaches.

---

## Short Passages

For short passages, it is usually better to read the question first and then read the passage.

This helps because the question tells you what to focus on.

For example, the question may ask about:

- the main idea
- the author's attitude
- the function of a sentence
- a specific detail
- an inference

If you read the question first, you can read the passage with a clear purpose instead of trying to memorize everything.

**Basic short-passage strategy:**

1. Read the question first.
2. Identify what the question is asking.
3. Read the passage with that target in mind.
4. Predict the answer before looking too closely at the choices.
5. Eliminate choices that are too strong, too broad, too narrow, or unsupported.

---

## Long Passages

For long passages, it is usually better to read the passage first and make a quick summary for each paragraph.

This may feel slow, but it is often the best use of time because a long passage can have three or four questions connected to it.

Sometimes it is better to spend more time on a long passage than to rush through it and lose several related questions.

For each paragraph, write or think a short summary.

The summary should include:

- the main point of the paragraph
- the role of the paragraph in the passage
- any contrast, shift, or disagreement
- important names, theories, dates, or examples
- the author's attitude if it is clear

**Example paragraph summary style:**

```
P1: introduces old theory and its weakness
P2: presents new evidence against old theory
P3: author says new theory is useful but incomplete
```

The goal is not to remember every detail. The goal is to build a map of the passage.

That map helps you quickly return to the correct place when answering questions.

---

## Specific Detail Questions in Long Passages

Long passages sometimes include multiple-selection questions or very specific detail questions.

These questions may ask about something that was not included in your paragraph summaries.

That is normal. A summary is a map, not a full copy of the passage.

When a question asks about a specific detail:

1. Identify which paragraph or section the question refers to.
2. Find the sentence that contains the relevant information.
3. Read the previous sentence.
4. Read the target sentence.
5. Read the next sentence.
6. Choose the answer based on that local context.

The previous and next sentences are important because GRE questions often test how a detail functions in context.

A detail may be:

- an example
- evidence for a claim
- a contrast with another view
- a limitation
- an objection
- a qualification

If you read only the target sentence, you may miss the author's actual purpose.

---

## Quick Strategy Table

| Passage Type | Best First Move | Why |
|--------------|-----------------|-----|
| Short passage | Read the question first | The question tells you what to look for |
| Long passage | Read the passage first and summarize each paragraph | Several questions may depend on the same passage |
| Long passage with specific detail question | Locate the relevant section and read around it | The answer may depend on nearby context |
| Multiple-selection detail question | Check the exact lines carefully | One small word can make an option wrong |

---

## GRE Tips and Common Traps

1. **Do not memorize the whole passage.**
   - Focus on structure, main ideas, and where details are located.

2. **For short passages, avoid over-reading.**
   - Let the question guide what matters.

3. **For long passages, do not skip the paragraph map.**
   - A few seconds summarizing each paragraph can save time across several questions.

4. **For detail questions, return to the passage.**
   - Do not answer from memory if the question is specific.

5. **Read around the referenced sentence.**
   - The sentence before and the sentence after often explain the purpose of the detail.

6. **Be careful with extreme answer choices.**
   - Words like `always`, `never`, `proves`, and `only` are often too strong unless the passage clearly supports them.
