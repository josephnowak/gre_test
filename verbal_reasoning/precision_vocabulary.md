# Precision Vocabulary Traps

GRE Verbal questions often punish answers that are merely reasonable. The correct word must match the exact logical job of the blank.

Some words look simple but have a formal or secondary meaning. In these questions, an ordinary answer may sound acceptable, but the GRE prefers the word that captures the precise relationship in the sentence.

---

## Core Strategy

Before choosing an answer, ask:

1. What exact idea does the sentence require?
2. Is the blank asking for tone, range, evidence, limitation, or contrast?
3. Does a familiar-looking word have a more precise academic meaning?
4. Is the tempting simple word too vague?

The trap is usually not that the easy word is impossible. The trap is that it is less exact.

---

## Example

As the study of literature expands to include multimedia texts, literary scholars commonly own film collections that are as _____ as their personal libraries, with the classics, foreign films, and science fiction all frequently explored in university English courses.

Options:

- Interesting
- Erudite
- idiosyncratic
- mundane
- catholic

Correct answer: **catholic**

Why: The clue is the wide range of examples: classics, foreign films, and science fiction. In this context, **catholic** means broad, wide-ranging, or universal in scope. **Interesting** is possible in everyday speech, but it does not explain the breadth of the collection.

---

## High-Value Words With Secondary Meanings

| Word | GRE meaning | Common trap |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| catholic | broad, wide-ranging, universal in interests | religious only |
| qualified | limited by reservations | professionally certified |
| telling | revealing, significant | speaking or talkative |
| checkered | mixed, with good and bad parts | visually patterned only |
| discriminating | selective, discerning | unfairly biased only |
| pedestrian | ordinary, dull, unimaginative | related to walking only |
| plastic | moldable, adaptable | synthetic material only |
| economical | concise or efficient | cheap only |

## Practice Questions

### 1

The critic's approval was _____: she praised the archive's ambition but warned that several conclusions rested on incomplete evidence.

- lavish
- qualified
- accidental
- hostile
- decorative

### 2

The mayor's silence after the report was _____, suggesting that the administration had not expected its own data to undermine the proposal.

- telling
- talkative
- routine
- generous
- opaque

### 3

The institute's record is _____: it has sponsored important discoveries, but it has also backed several projects later judged unsound.

- spotless
- checkered
- ceremonial
- narrow
- erudite

### 4

Because the committee had to choose among many strong applications, it was deliberately _____, funding only projects with clear methods and realistic timelines.

- discriminating
- prejudiced
- indifferent
- ornate
- temporary

### 5

The essay was grammatically polished but intellectually _____, offering familiar examples and a conclusion that added little to the debate.

- pedestrian
- walkable
- radical
- comprehensive
- zealous

### 6

The genre proved surprisingly _____, absorbing features of memoir, investigative journalism, and philosophical dialogue.

- plastic
- synthetic
- rigid
- perfunctory
- obsolete

### 7

The editor's style is _____: few sentences are long, and no example is included unless it advances the argument.

- economical
- inexpensive
- excessive
- sentimental
- erratic

---

## Answer Key

1. **qualified**: The approval is limited by a warning.
2. **telling**: The silence reveals something important.
3. **checkered**: The record includes both successes and failures.
4. **discriminating**: The committee is selective and judicious, not biased.
5. **pedestrian**: The essay is ordinary and unimaginative.
6. **plastic**: The genre is moldable and adaptable.
7. **economical**: The style is concise and efficient.
