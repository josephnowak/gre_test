### Adrift

- **Part of speech:** adj/adv
- **Definition:** floating without control or direction; figuratively, lacking purpose, guidance, or stability
- **Synonyms:** aimless, floating, directionless
- **Example:** After the company closed, many workers felt adrift.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list

### Aid

- **Part of speech:** n/v
- **Definition:** help or assistance; as a verb, to help. Aid is often slightly more formal than help and can suggest material, official, or practical support
- **Synonyms:** help, assistance, support
- **Example:** The grant will aid students who cannot afford test preparation materials.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list

### Concealed

- **Part of speech:** adj/v
- **Definition:** hidden from view or knowledge; also the past form of conceal
- **Synonyms:** hidden, covered, disguised
- **Example:** The report revealed several concealed costs in the contract.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list

### Ease

- **Part of speech:** n/v
- **Definition:** comfort or freedom from difficulty; as a verb, to reduce pain, pressure, or difficulty
- **Synonyms:** comfort, simplicity, alleviate
- **Example:** The clear instructions eased the students into the difficult assignment.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list

### Exempt

- **Part of speech:** adj/v
- **Definition:** free from a duty, rule, or requirement; as a verb, to release someone from such a requirement
- **Synonyms:** excused, immune, released
- **Example:** Students with prior credit were exempt from the introductory course.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list

### Juxtaposed

- **Part of speech:** adj/v
- **Definition:** placed side by side for comparison or contrast; the past form of juxtapose
- **Synonyms:** contrasted, compared, placed together
- **Example:** The poem juxtaposed images of luxury and poverty to sharpen its social criticism.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list

### Lavish

- **Part of speech:** adj/v
- **Definition:** luxurious, abundant, or extravagant; as a verb, to give generously
- **Synonyms:** luxurious, extravagant, abundant
- **Example:** The host prepared a lavish dinner for the visiting scholars.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list

### Pinpoint

- **Part of speech:** v/n/adj
- **Definition:** to identify something exactly; also a very small point or something extremely precise
- **Synonyms:** identify, locate, determine exactly
- **Example:** The analyst tried to pinpoint the cause of the sudden decline.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list

### Settled

- **Part of speech:** adj/v
- **Definition:** stable, resolved, or established; also the past form of settle
- **Synonyms:** resolved, established, stable
- **Example:** Once the dispute was settled, the team returned to work.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list

### Sinking

- **Part of speech:** adj/n/v
- **Definition:** moving downward, declining, or becoming submerged; also a downward feeling or process
- **Synonyms:** descending, declining, submerging
- **Example:** The sinking ship forced the crew to abandon their cargo.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list

### Sledding

- **Part of speech:** n/v
- **Definition:** the activity of riding on a sled; also the gerund form of sled
- **Synonyms:** sled riding, sliding
- **Example:** The children spent the afternoon sledding down the snowy hill.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list

### Utmost

- **Part of speech:** adj/n
- **Definition:** greatest or highest in degree; as a noun, the maximum possible effort or extent
- **Synonyms:** maximum, greatest, highest
- **Example:** The surgeon treated the case with the utmost care.
- **Source:** Supplemental word list
